## Stand-Up Paddleboard (SUP) Racing

Pablo is a competitive SUP racer on a 14' carbon race board, tackling some of the most demanding endurance events in the sport:

- **Chattajack** (Chattanooga, TN) — 31-mile race down the Tennessee River. One of the most prestigious endurance SUP events in the world.
- **Tahoe Cup Lake Crossing** (Lake Tahoe, CA/NV) — 22-mile open-water crossing of Lake Tahoe on a 14' board. A full day on the water.
- **Sea Trek Regatta** (Sausalito, CA) — Annual race with three distance categories. Pablo has completed all three lengths and podiumed in his category across all of them.
- **Battle of the Bay** (San Francisco Bay) — Annual Bay Area race and a regular on his calendar.
- **Redwood Water Sports local races** (Redwood City, CA) — Local community events close to home.

SUP racing is where The Paddle Games came from. Pablo built the entire platform — leaderboards, gamification, geo-spatial challenges — because the Bay Area paddle sports community needed it and nobody else was building it.

## Kiteboarding / Kitesurfing

Pablo picked up kiteboarding around 2013, right before moving to the US. He bought his first kite during a business trip to Orlando while working on the Disney Parks project — a friend from his hometown in Argentina had taught him the basics and he never looked back.

In Los Angeles he started riding at Belmont Shores in Long Beach. The wind wasn't great, but the community was — he made lifelong friends there, including a crew that did a kite trip to Turks and Caicos in 2025 to celebrate a wedding.

When he moved to the Bay Area, things got serious. The wind up here is consistent and the spots are world-class:

- **Crissy Field** (San Francisco) — the classic Bay Area kite spot
- **Ocean Beach** (San Francisco) — bigger swells, more power
- **3rd Avenue** (San Mateo) — reliable flat-water spot
- **Half Moon Bay** — when the storms roll in
- **Waddell Beach** (Santa Cruz) — his favorite. Wave riding with a kite on a proper surf break. This is where flat-water kiting ends and real kitesurfing begins.

### Naish Team Rider (Ambassador) — since 2020

Pablo has been riding Naish gear since day one — the friend who taught him was a Naish devotee and it stuck. After years of consistently riding their equipment and posting sessions on Instagram, some established ambassadors from the Bay Area noticed and invited him onto the team.

As an ambassador he creates content (GoPro POV edits, session clips), helps with demo days and gear demos for prospective riders, and represents the brand in the Bay Area water sports community. In return he gets gear discounts and invitations to Naish tech talks and product previews.

Naish is one of the biggest names in water sports — being on their team as a non-professional rider is a recognition of his commitment to the sport and the community.

## CrossFit

Pablo got into CrossFit as a way to stay fit for his water sports — it fills the gaps when there's no wind or it's too rainy for a paddle session. He got pretty into it though. Competed in small local competitions while training at CrossFit Flagship in San Francisco, did the CrossFit Open a couple of times, and can bang out solid muscle ups. Currently training at CrossFit Incredible in Menlo Park.

## Road Cycling

Pablo got into cycling during the pandemic after watching a YouTube video of someone descending Hawk Hill in the Marin Headlands. That became the goal. He got the bike, got the gear (yes, including the bibs), and started tracking everything on Strava — the same app he'd later use as the backbone for The Paddle Games.

He got competitive about it fast. While chasing a personal record descending Hawk Hill, he lost control at 70 km/h and hit a tree. Walked away with just scratches, but it was a close call. The kind of story that makes you appreciate helmets.

## The Paddle Games — Where Sport Meets Engineering

The Paddle Games is Pablo's personal project that sits at the intersection of his two worlds — engineering and water sports. It's a full-stack leaderboard platform for Bay Area paddle sports (kayaking, SUP, canoeing, surfing) with Strava integration, a data-driven gamification engine, and geo-spatial challenges. Currently serving 100+ active paddlers with a growing Instagram community (@the.paddle.games).

This isn't a side project in the "I built a todo app" sense. It's 6 microservices on GCP, event-driven architecture with Pub/Sub, a React 19 frontend, and a gamification engine where all scoring rules and challenges are defined as data. He designed, built, and launched it solo.
