Alongside my day job, I build and run my own products, and I use them to try new ideas in AI engineering before I bring them to work. Everything here is designed, built, shipped and operated by me, from the infrastructure to the people using it.

The Paddle Games is a leaderboard and challenge platform for Bay Area paddlers, live on the web and as a native iPhone app on the App Store. It syncs activities from Strava, scores them with a data-driven rules engine and runs route challenges with personal records, on seven event-driven services on Google Cloud, with close to 100 paddlers.

Project Batcave is my personal multi-agent system on the Claude Agent SDK: a router and three agents for memory, nutrition and training, reached through Telegram voice, an iPhone and Apple Watch app and the web, deployed on Cloud Run behind eval gates.

This portfolio is an experiment too. It's a Next.js site with a RAG agent, Guillermo, that answers questions about my work from a vector index of everything published here and replies with interactive cards instead of plain text, books calls, and reports errors and usage through Sentry and PostHog.

I also use these projects to test new agentic workflows in Claude Code. Plugins with skills and subagents write, score and publish my blog posts with an independent reviewer that can send a draft back, draft the social posts, scout where new Paddle Games challenges belong from real GPS data, and triage and fix bugs by opening pull requests for me to review. Each one is a small, real test of how far agents can go with the right guardrails, and the lessons carry straight into my day job.
