Stellarus is a healthcare solutions company that builds products and services for health plans, helping them automate operations, reduce costs and improve the experience for their members. Its solutions launched first with Blue Shield of California, as part of the Ascendiun family of companies.

I joined as the AI engineer on the team that took over Blue Shield of California's applications from an outsourced vendor. My first job was AI adoption. I designed the team's AI Task Force and lead it on the technical side. I ran a discovery of every AI tool already in use, and documented it in Confluence through a Codex plugin I built to publish straight from the codebase. I also laid out the structure of the team's Confluence space, and built proofs of concept for shared Codex and Copilot plugins so agent skills live in one place instead of being copied into random branches across scattered repos. The Task Force has since grown into a program where every engineer spends 30% of their time on AI work, and I help them design the use cases they bring.

When new leadership ran a Wiz security scan, more than 5,000 vulnerability tickets landed on the team with no plan to work through them. I exported the full list, grouped it into the main families of issues, and built, on my own, a campaign system where coding agents fix vulnerabilities in bulk: each fix gets a baseline build before and after, runs in its own git worktree, and arrives as a pull request explaining what changed and why. The agents opened more than 100 pull requests covering thousands of tickets. Findings that lived in cloud configuration instead of code went to the cloud and DevOps teams as Ansible playbooks I worked out with them. The system tracks every ticket's status, keeps it in sync with Jira, and shows it in a React dashboard.
